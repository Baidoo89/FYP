# User Acceptance Testing (UAT) Materials

Referenced from Chapter Four, §4.13.7. This document is the actual instrument to run with real participants before final submission — not a description of results, since no results exist yet. Once the session(s) below are run, replace §4.13.7 in Chapter Four with the real findings (participant count, roles, task completion, and a short thematic summary of the feedback collected), and update Chapter Five §5.6 item 7 accordingly.

## How to run this

1. **Recruit 3–5 participants**, ideally covering at least three different roles (e.g. one lecturer, one HOD/Dean or HR staff member, one person unfamiliar with the system). They do not need to be actual GCTU staff for a prototype evaluation, but should be people who can plausibly judge whether the workflow makes sense for a university promotion process (classmates, faculty, or staff who agree to help).
2. **Use the staging/demo environment**, not real GCTU data — consistent with Chapter One §1.9 (no real institutional data was used in this study). Seed a fresh demo account per participant beforehand.
3. **Sit with each participant individually** (or over a screen share), hand them the relevant task script below for their assigned role, and ask them to narrate what they're doing as they go ("think aloud"). Do not help unless they are fully stuck — where they hesitate or get it wrong is exactly the useful signal.
4. **Time-box each session to 15–20 minutes.**
5. **Immediately after**, have them fill in the feedback form for their role while it's fresh (or ask the questions verbally and record their answers yourself).
6. **Consent and data handling**: tell each participant plainly that this is a student project evaluation, not an official GCTU system; that their feedback will be summarised (not attributed by name) in an academic report; and that they can stop at any time. Do not collect anything beyond their role/first name and their answers — no need for staff ID, email, or other identifying data for this exercise.

## Task Script — Lecturer

Give the participant a demo lecturer account (freshly registered or pre-seeded) and ask them to, without further instruction:

1. Log in.
2. Start a new promotion application (select a current rank and a target rank).
3. Upload one evidence file under the Teaching category, and one under the Research category.
4. Find out what evidence categories are still missing before they can submit.
5. Submit the application.
6. Find out what status their application is currently in, and what happens next.

**Observe**: did they find the evidence-category upload flow on their own? Did they understand which categories were required vs. optional? Did they know what to do after submitting?

## Task Script — HOD / Dean

Give the participant a demo HOD/Dean account with at least one pending application in their queue.

1. Log in and find the applications waiting for their review.
2. Open one application and review the uploaded evidence.
3. Leave a review comment.
4. Decide whether to forward it to HR or return it for correction, and do so.

**Observe**: was the distinction between "academic review" (their role) and "document verification" (HR's role) clear from the interface? Did they understand what forwarding vs. returning would do before clicking it?

## Task Script — HR Administrator

Give the participant a demo HR account with at least one application forwarded from a department, with evidence uploaded.

1. Log in and find the application awaiting verification.
2. Verify each uploaded document one at a time.
3. Once all required documents are verified, find out what happened to the application's eligibility status.
4. Explain, in their own words, what the "Criteria Score" and "Eligible" status mean and how they differ.

**Observe**: did they correctly understand that the criteria score is about evidence completeness, not a performance grade? This is the single most important thing to check given the terminology concern raised during supervisor review (Chapter Four, §4.9.1).

## Task Script — Committee Reviewer

Give the participant a demo Committee Reviewer account with at least one eligible application in their queue.

1. Log in and open the eligible application.
2. Review the evidence and the eligibility recommendation.
3. Record a recommendation (Recommended / Not Recommended / Requires Further Review) with a justification comment.

**Observe**: did they understand that their recommendation is not the final promotion decision? Did the interface make that clear without being told?

## Feedback Form (all roles)

Ask these after the task script, either as a short written form or verbally (record answers):

1. On a scale of 1–5 (1 = very difficult, 5 = very easy), how easy was it to complete the task(s) you were given?
2. Was there any point where you were unsure what to do next? If so, where?
3. Was there any label, status, or term on screen that was confusing or unclear? (Prompt specifically: did "Criteria Score" make sense, or did it look like a performance grade at first glance?)
4. Did the system feel trustworthy for something as important as a promotion application? Why or why not?
5. Is there anything you expected the system to do that it didn't?
6. Any other comments.

## Recording results

For each participant, record: role played, task completion (completed unaided / completed with help / did not complete), answers to the six questions above, and any notable quotes or observations. Once 3–5 sessions are complete, summarise across participants (e.g. "4 of 5 participants completed the HR verification task unaided; the one who struggled was unsure whether verifying a document also submitted it") rather than reporting each session individually — that summary, plus this instrument, is what belongs in Chapter Four §4.13.7.
